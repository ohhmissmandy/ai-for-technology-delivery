# Does This Decision Actually Hold Up?

Use this when a project or technology decision feels important enough to pressure-test before committing to it.

## Prompt

Review the decision below and challenge it objectively.

First, separate what we know from what we're assuming. Look at the evidence for and against the decision, what important information is missing, who will be affected, what other options exist, what happens downstream, and how hard the decision would be to reverse.

Identify:

* the strongest reasons to make the decision
* the strongest legitimate reasons not to
* assumptions the decision depends on
* consequences we may be underestimating
* dependencies or follow-on decisions it creates
* what would make the decision difficult or expensive to reverse
* what information would materially change the recommendation

If there are realistic alternatives, compare them based on the factors that actually matter here.

Don't invent a counterargument just to make the analysis look balanced.

Finish with:

**What the evidence supports**

**What still needs to be answered**

**Recommendation**

**Confidence in the recommendation and why**

## What to provide

Paste the proposed decision, available options, supporting information, constraints, assumptions, stakeholder concerns, and any known consequences.
