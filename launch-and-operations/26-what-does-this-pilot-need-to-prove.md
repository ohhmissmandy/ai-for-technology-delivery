# What Does This Pilot Need to Prove?

Use this when you are planning a pilot and need to be clear about what it should actually tell you before a broader rollout.

## Prompt

Review the proposed pilot and help define what it needs to prove.

Start with the decision the pilot is supposed to support. What will we need to know at the end to decide whether to move forward, make changes, expand the pilot, or stop?

Identify the most important things to validate, such as:

* whether the solution works in the real environment
* whether users can complete the intended work
* whether integrations and data behave as expected
* whether the process works operationally
* whether support can handle it
* whether users adopt it as expected
* whether expected benefits appear realistic
* whether known risks are manageable
* whether the solution can reasonably scale

Only include measures that matter to the decision.

For each pilot objective, define:

* what we are trying to learn
* how we will measure or observe it
* what evidence we need
* what would count as success
* what would make us reconsider the approach

Also identify the pilot population, duration, dependencies, support needs, and any conditions that could make the results misleading.

Do not define pilot success as simply completing the pilot without a major failure.

Finish with:

**What the pilot needs to prove**

**Evidence we need**

**Decision we should be able to make afterward**

**What would make the pilot inconclusive**

## What to provide

Paste the proposed solution, pilot scope, intended users, business goals, known risks, success measures, rollout plans, and any current pilot assumptions.
