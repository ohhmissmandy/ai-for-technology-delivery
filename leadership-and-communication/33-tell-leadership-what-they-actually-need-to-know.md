# Tell Leadership What They Actually Need to Know

Use this when you need to turn detailed project information into an executive-level update without hiding the things that matter.

## Prompt

Review the project information below and create a concise leadership update.

Focus on:

* overall status
* what changed since the last update
* meaningful progress
* major risks or issues
* decisions leadership needs to make
* dependencies that need leadership attention
* changes to scope, timing, cost, or expected outcome
* anything likely to surprise leadership later if it is not raised now

Separate normal project noise from information that actually requires executive attention.

Do not make the update sound healthier or worse than the evidence supports.

For every item that needs leadership involvement, make the ask clear.

Use plain language. Avoid unnecessary project-management terminology and technical detail unless it matters to the decision.

Finish with:

**What leadership needs to know**

**What leadership needs to decide or do**

**What we're watching**

## What to provide

Paste the current project status, recent progress, milestones, risks, issues, decisions, dependencies, changes, and any previous leadership commitments or expectations.
