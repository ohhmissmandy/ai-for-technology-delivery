# AI for Technology Delivery

Practical AI prompts for technology delivery, from messy intake through launch, adoption, and operational ownership.

I built this collection around the parts of technology work where simply generating a checklist is not enough. The prompts are meant to help organize incomplete information, challenge assumptions, find gaps, pressure-test plans, and improve the questions people ask before making a decision.

They are not meant to replace the person accountable for the work.

The collection covers planning, requirements, UAT, risk, deployment, training, adoption, operational readiness, executive communication, process improvement, and responsible AI.

## A few rules I use with all of these

### Don't invent what isn't there

If information is missing, identify the gap instead of filling it in.

### Separate what we know from what we're assuming

Especially when a recommendation or decision depends on it.

### Challenge the work, but don't manufacture problems

If the plan holds up, say so.

### Use AI to improve the questions and analysis, not replace accountability

A person still owns the decision.

### Protect sensitive information

Use approved AI systems and follow your organization's data-handling rules. Do not provide confidential, proprietary, regulated, customer, employee, or security-sensitive information unless that use has been approved.

AI-generated analysis should be reviewed before it is used for project, operational, governance, or business decisions.

## Featured prompts

A few good places to start:

### [Turn a Messy Idea Into a Real Plan](getting-started/01-turn-a-messy-idea-into-a-real-plan.md)

Take rough notes, requests, dates, assumptions, and incomplete information and turn them into a delivery view without quietly making up the missing pieces.

### [Learn Enough to Lead the Implementation](getting-started/03-learn-enough-to-lead-the-implementation.md)

Get practical context on an unfamiliar technology so you can lead the implementation, ask better questions, and know what should stay with the technical SMEs.

### [Can This Timeline Actually Work?](planning-and-decisions/06-can-this-timeline-actually-work.md)

Pressure-test sequencing, resources, lead times, approvals, testing windows, and dependencies before treating a schedule as committed.

### [What Would We See Before It Goes Wrong?](planning-and-decisions/10-what-would-we-see-before-it-goes-wrong.md)

Turn known risks into observable early-warning signals so the team has a chance to act before the final outcome is affected.

### [This Project Is Going Sideways. Now What?](planning-and-decisions/12-this-project-is-going-sideways-now-what.md)

Separate symptoms from root causes and build realistic recovery options when the original plan is no longer credible.

### [Does This Decision Actually Hold Up?](planning-and-decisions/13-does-this-decision-actually-hold-up.md)

Test a proposed decision against the evidence, assumptions, alternatives, downstream effects, and reversibility before committing.

### [Build UAT That Tests Real Life](requirements-and-uat/17-build-uat-that-tests-real-life.md)

Build UAT around real workflows, business rules, exceptions, user roles, and evidence instead of relying on happy-path scripts.

### [Are We Actually Ready to Launch?](launch-and-operations/22-are-we-actually-ready-to-launch.md)

Look beyond completed tasks and assess whether the technical, business, operational, vendor, training, support, and contingency pieces are actually ready.

### [What Does This Pilot Need to Prove?](launch-and-operations/26-what-does-this-pilot-need-to-prove.md)

Design a pilot around evidence and decision-making instead of simply putting the solution in front of a smaller group.

### [Is This Really a Training Problem?](training-and-adoption/30-is-this-really-a-training-problem.md)

Separate real knowledge gaps from usability, process, configuration, workload, support, or adoption problems.

### [Can Operations Own This After We Leave?](launch-and-operations/32-can-operations-own-this-after-we-leave.md)

Check whether the receiving team can actually support and operate the solution without depending on the project team indefinitely.

### [Where Does a Human Still Need to Be Involved?](responsible-ai/34-where-does-a-human-still-need-to-be-involved.md)

Evaluate AI-enabled workflows based on consequence, reversibility, judgment, accountability, data quality, and the kind of human involvement that actually adds value.
